The brief I set myself

Most portfolio dashboards show a clean number and stop there. I wanted to show the mess underneath too, since real retail data always has orphaned foreign keys, invalid prices, and missing currency codes. Hiding that doesn't make a report more useful, just less honest. So GulfCart has a page most tutorial projects skip: Data Quality.

Executive Summary

The landing page answers what a stakeholder actually opens the report to ask: what did we make, how healthy is the margin, and how did that break down by channel and category. A metric selector lets the same visuals flip between Net Revenue, Gross Revenue, Net Revenue after Returns, Gross Margin, Orders, Units, and Average Order Value.

What's modelled here

  • Net Revenue, Gross Margin, Margin %, Return Rate (Value) as core measures, all filterable by channel and year
  • A disconnected measure-selector table driving the KPI card and trend chart, so one visual covers seven metrics
  • A category table combining Net Revenue, Target, Attainment %, Margin %, and Return Rate for quick scanning

Why it's built this way

A metric-selector pattern (bind a slicer to a disconnected table, then SWITCH on the selected value inside one measure) stops the report sprawling into a dozen near-identical charts. It's the same trick I used in the BI Gauntlet build, reused once I knew it worked.

Category & Product

Drills into where margin is actually being won or lost, by category and by return behaviour, which quietly eats into margin in ways a top-line revenue number won't show.

Electronics and Fashion carry the biggest revenue share, but Sports has the widest negative target variance and the highest return rate, the kind of gap a single blended margin number would hide. Order lines that couldn't be matched to a product sit under an Unknown category with their revenue kept intact, following the Kimball "unknown member" pattern instead of just excluding them.

Customer

RFM segmentation and a full month-by-month cohort retention matrix, because "new vs returning revenue" alone doesn't tell you if the business is actually keeping customers.

RFM segments are scored at 180 days recency, 5 orders frequency, and AED 30,000 monetary value, thresholds written right on the page instead of buried in a measure someone would need to reverse-engineer. The cohort table tracks retention for every acquisition year and quarter against every month after, which is what actually shows whether retention is improving year over year (2024 and 2025 cohorts consistently beat 2022's, month for month).

Data Quality

This is the page I'm most glad I built. 2,497 rows got excluded from the model during cleaning, and every one carries a reason code instead of just disappearing.

What was rejected, and why

  • 1,038 rows: orphaned order IDs referencing an order that wasn't in the orders table
  • 836 rows: invalid quantities or unit prices
  • 407 orders: missing currency codes or dates that wouldn't parse
  • 177 returns: invalid return dates, or returns dated before their order
  • 78 tier-history rows: invalid validity date ranges

Separately, 810 order lines couldn't be matched to a product and 563 lines across 258 orders couldn't be matched to a customer. Those weren't excluded, just kept under an unknown-member key so their revenue stays in the totals. Dropping them would've quietly understated revenue for a reason that has nothing to do with the business.

Power Query approach

Each source table gets validated on ingest, and failing rows get routed to a rejects table with a reason code rather than filtered out silently. That reason code is what drives the chart on this page, so the report is a direct read of what the transformation layer actually did.

What I'd extend next

  • Add a reconciliation KPI (rejected-row value as a percentage of total order value) to tie the data-quality page back to the executive summary
  • Push the RFM scoring thresholds into parameters so they can be tuned without editing DAX
  • Add a returns-reason breakdown alongside the existing return-rate visuals
