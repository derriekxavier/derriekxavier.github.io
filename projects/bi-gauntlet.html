Why one page is the harder brief

A multi-page report can bury a weak measure behind three strong ones. One page can't. The rolling average trend, the ABC split, the year-over-year movement, and the target attainment all had to work individually and hold together as a set, since a reviewer sees all of it at once.

The page

What's on it

  • A metric selector that switches the KPI card and trend line between Revenue YoY %, LFL Revenue YoY %, Net Revenue, and Attainment %
  • A 28-day rolling average trend, smoothing daily noise without hiding the underlying shape
  • ABC classification of revenue by class (A: 69.5%, B: 20.4%, C: 10.2%), a Pareto-style view of where revenue actually concentrates
  • Net Revenue vs Target by fiscal year, next to Revenue YoY % by fiscal year, so growth and attainment read together

The metric-selector technique

A disconnected table lists the available metrics, and a slicer against that table feeds one measure that switches its calculation with SWITCH. One visual, one measure, no duplicated pages per metric. I reused and extended this pattern in the GulfCart build afterward.

Like-for-like vs headline growth

Headline Revenue YoY sits at 32.2%, but LFL Revenue YoY, which strips out new stores or channels opened during the period, comes in at 2.0%. Showing both instead of just the flattering headline number is the difference between a dashboard that looks good and one a finance stakeholder can actually trust.

What I'd extend next

  • Add a drill-through from the ABC classification into product or SKU-level detail
  • Show the LFL calculation logic as a visible note on the page, not just in the measure
  • Add a country or region breakdown alongside the existing currency and fiscal-year slicers
